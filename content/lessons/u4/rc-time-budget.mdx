## The arithmetic

Thirty-five minutes, four passages, twenty-six to twenty-eight questions. That is 8:45 a passage with nothing to spare, so plan on 8:30 and let the balance absorb one hard set. Within a passage: 3 to 4 minutes reading, 4:30 to 5:30 on six or seven questions. Call it 45 seconds a question, which is enough when the map is good and not enough when it is not.

| Checkpoint | Clock (counting down from 35:00) |
|---|---|
| End of passage 1 | 26:00 or later |
| End of passage 2 | 17:30 |
| End of passage 3 | 9:00 |
| Last question answered | 0:30, to fill flagged blanks |

Check the clock at passage ends, not mid-passage.

## The 40 percent rule

Spend at least 40 percent of a passage's time on the passage: 3:30 of 8:45. Under pressure the temptation is to skim in two minutes and "make it up on the questions." It never balances. A thin read costs 15 to 20 seconds on every question, because each one sends you back to rebuild what the map should have held, and seven questions times 20 seconds exceeds the ninety seconds you saved. The read is the one part of a passage you do once. Do it properly.

Properly means the previous lesson: a summary per paragraph, the viewpoints, the author's stance, the pivots. It does not mean rereading a sentence until it feels understood. If a sentence resists on the first pass, note its paragraph and keep moving; its job is usually clear from the paragraph around it.

## Passage order

Do the passages in the order you read them best, not the order printed. In the first thirty seconds, read the opening sentence or two of each and note subject and style. Put the one that reads fastest for you first and the one you dread last. Every question is worth one point; the hard passage should get whatever time remains, not the time the easy ones needed.

Do not spend more than thirty seconds choosing. A wrong order costs less than a long deliberation.

## Answer from the map, or go back?

Two kinds of question, two procedures.

| Answer from the map | Go back to the text |
|---|---|
| main point, primary purpose | stated (the stem names the detail) |
| organization, purpose of a paragraph | meaning in context (reread the sentence) |
| author's attitude, author's view of the thesis | purpose in context (the sentence and the one before it) |
| relationship between passages | other's perspective, when it is a specific claim |

Left column: prephrase from the map, find the match, confirm against the text only if two choices survive. Right column: go back *first*, before the choices, and read the sentence with its neighbors. The key paraphrases what is there, and reading the choices first lets a paraphrase that is slightly off feel familiar.

## When a question runs long

At ninety seconds, pick the best survivor, flag it, and move. The questions you have not reached are worth more than the one you are polishing. When you return, return to the text, not to the choices.

<KeyIdea>
Budget 8:30 a passage and at least 3:30 of it reading. Choose passage order in thirty seconds, answer structure questions from the map, go to the text before the choices on detail questions, and leave any question that passes ninety seconds.
</KeyIdea>
