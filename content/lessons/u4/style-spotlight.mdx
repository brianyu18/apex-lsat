## What the style looks like

One figure, one body of work, or one book. The first paragraph gives the received judgment and often how it was formed ("by critics who heard the quartets once"). The middle shows what the received judgment missed. The end files the subject under a new heading. Humanities passages use this shape most, but it also carries scientists, jurists and movements.

Spotlight passages are dense with retrievable detail: names, dates, numbers of works, which one failed. Those details feed the stated questions. Do not memorize them; note which paragraph holds them.

## How the author's position is signaled

- **Verbs about the received view.** *Has been treated as*, *the judgment was formed early*, *repeated more often than tested*. These report; they also judge.
- **Praise with a ceiling.** *Deserve not rehabilitation but reclassification*. *A failed experiment is not a retreat.* The author admires and withholds in the same sentence, and the attitude key must do both.
- **Concessions about the subject.** A spotlight author who admits the subject's failures is building credibility for the reassessment. The admitted failure is a frequent purpose-in-context target.

## Which questions it attracts

**Author's attitude.** *The author's attitude toward Marrow's late quartets can most accurately be described as...* Calibrate. The author calls them experiments, denies they are masterpieces, calls the third a failure, and still insists they were misjudged. *Qualified appreciation* fits. *Enthusiastic admiration* overshoots on "which they are not." *Dismissive* reverses the passage. And note there are two targets for attitude here: toward the quartets (qualified appreciation) and toward the critical tradition (pointed disapproval). Read which one the stem names.

**Stated.** *According to the passage, the third quartet...* The stem names the detail; the map says paragraph three; the sentence says it dismantles its source so thoroughly that what remains is more demonstration than music. The key paraphrases that. A trap says the third quartet "fails to quote its source," which swaps the nearby concept.

**Purpose in context.** *The author mentions that Marrow left no account of her intentions primarily in order to...* Function: to concede a limit on the evidence before arguing the distinction holds anyway. Not "to explain why critics misjudged the works"; the passage attributes that to hearing them once.

## The level-4 version

The hard spotlight passage reassesses a reassessment: an earlier rehabilitation went too far, and the author lands between the original critics and the rehabilitators. Three attitudes, each calibrated. Map the author's stance toward each camp separately, and expect the attitude key to be a two-word phrase with a qualifier in it.

<KeyIdea>
In a spotlight passage, the author's attitude is set by the words that withhold as much as by the words that praise. Record both, know which paragraph holds each detail, and read the stem for which target the attitude question is about.
</KeyIdea>
