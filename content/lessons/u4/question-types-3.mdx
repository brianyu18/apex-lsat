## The stems

> *Which one of the following is most analogous to X as described in the passage?*
> *Which one of the following would the author most likely consider an example of...?*
> *Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the author's argument...?*
> *Which one of the following principles underlies the author's reasoning...?*
> *Which one of the following most accurately describes the organization of the passage?*

Four or five per section. Each is an LR skill you already own, applied to an argument you must extract first. The extraction is the work: before the choices, write the author's argument in LR form, conclusion, evidence, and the move between them.

|RC type|LR skill|What you extract|
|---|---|---|
|Analogy|Parallel reasoning|the abstract structure of the thing named|
|Application|Principle application|the author's criterion|
|Weaken / strengthen / evaluate|Weaken, Strengthen, Evaluate|the conclusion and the fact it rests on|
|Principle|Principle (identify)|the rule the judgment presupposes|
|Organization|Argument structure|the sequence of paragraph roles|

## Analogy: match the structure, drop the subject

Reduce the named thing to a relation with the nouns removed. The Varenne fairs: *a two-function institution collapsed when the less visible function was displaced, and observers blamed the visible one.* Now the key can be about a newspaper or a hospital. The trap shares the subject and breaks the relation. The subject is noise.

## Application: run the criterion on a new case

Write the author's test in one sentence first: *a constraint should be justified by the party it serves.* Run each case through it. The trap resembles the passage's examples on the surface and fails the test, or satisfies a different paragraph's claim.

## Weaken and strengthen: target the discriminating fact

The author's argument usually rests on one fact that does the sorting: the chronology, the neighboring islands. A weakener removes or neutralizes that fact. A strengthener adds a prediction the author's view makes and the rival does not. A choice that contradicts the conclusion without touching the reasoning is weak; one about a point the passage never contested is noise. Evaluate asks for the fact whose answer would move your confidence; a fact that comes out the same under both views evaluates nothing.

## Principle: the rule at the author's altitude

General enough to cover the author's judgment, no more general than the text supports. State it from the author's reasons, then match. Traps run both ways: a rule so broad it condemns things the author accepts, and the specific remedy with "should" in front of it.

## Organization: verify every clause

The key is your role map as verbs. Check each clause in order; a choice dies at the first clause that misdescribes a paragraph. Traps get the opening right because the opening is what everyone remembers.

## The level-4 version

Hard analogy questions name an abstract relation ("the relationship between the manuals and the practice they describe") rather than a thing. Hard weaken questions target a sub-argument in a middle paragraph, so extract the right argument before weakening it. Hard principle questions offer two rules that both fit the conclusion and differ in the reason. Name the conclusion, the evidence, the move, then judge.

<KeyIdea>
These five types are LR with a long stimulus. Extract the author's argument first, then match structure for analogy, run the criterion for application, hit the discriminating fact for weaken and strengthen, state the rule at the author's altitude for principle, and verify every clause for organization.
</KeyIdea>
