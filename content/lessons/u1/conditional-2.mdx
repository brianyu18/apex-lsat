## Four groups

Every conditional indicator on the LSAT falls into one of four groups. Each group has a single translation rule.

### Group 1 — introduces the sufficient condition
**if, when, whenever, every, all, any, each, in order to, people who**

The thing right after the word goes on the **left** of the arrow.

> *Every member who attended received a certificate.* → attended → certificate

### Group 2 — introduces the necessary condition
**only, only if, only when, must, requires, needs, depends on, essential, precondition**

The thing right after the word goes on the **right** of the arrow.

> *The grant is awarded only to projects with a public component.* → grant → public component
> *Admission requires a portfolio.* → admission → portfolio

### Group 3 — "unless / until / without / except": negate the other side
**unless, until, without, except, besides**

Take the clause introduced by the word and put it on the **right** of the arrow. Take the *other* clause, **negate** it, and put it on the left.

> *No refund unless you have a receipt.* → refund → receipt
> *The bridge cannot open without an inspection.* → open → inspection
> *Everyone except staff must register.* → ¬staff → register

Mnemonic: *unless* = *if not*. "No refund unless receipt" = "if no receipt, no refund" = refund → receipt.

### Group 4 — "no / none / never / cannot": negate the necessary
**no, none, never, cannot, not both**

The sufficient is whatever follows the word; the necessary is the *negation* of the other side.

> *No employee may hold two offices.* → employee → ¬two offices
> *Reptiles never nurse their young.* → reptile → ¬nurse

<KeyIdea>
Groups 1 and 2 place a condition. Groups 3 and 4 place a condition *and flip a sign*. The flip is what people forget under time.
</KeyIdea>

## Two sentences that look alike

- *"Only members may vote."* → vote → member (Group 2: "only" marks the necessary).
- *"The only people who vote are members."* → vote → member. Same thing — "the only" still marks the necessary, which is what comes *after* it: being a member is required.

But:

- *"Members are the only people who vote."* → vote → member again. The necessary condition is *member* regardless of word order. Ask: what is required for what? Voting requires membership.

## Common pairings that trip people

| Phrase | Arrow |
|---|---|
| A is necessary for B | B → A |
| A is sufficient for B | A → B |
| A guarantees B | A → B |
| A depends on B | A → B |
| Not A unless B | A → B |
| A if and only if B | A → B and B → A |
