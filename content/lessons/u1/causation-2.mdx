## The move set

For a conclusion *"A causes B"*:

| To weaken | To strengthen |
|---|---|
| Offer an alternative cause C | Rule out C |
| Show B could cause A | Show A preceded B, or B can't cause A |
| Show the correlation is unreliable (sample, chance) | Show it holds across many cases / randomized |
| Show A present, B absent (cause without effect) | Show A absent, B absent (no cause, no effect) |
| Break the mechanism | Supply a mechanism |

The two "cause/effect" rows are the most powerful and the least expected. *"In towns that did not install roundabouts, crash severity fell just as much"* is devastating — the effect appears without the cause. *"Towns that removed roundabouts saw severity rise again"* is strong support.

## Grading "most"

The stem says *most* weakens or *most* strengthens, so some wrong answers do move the dial slightly. Rank by:

1. **Does it address the causal link, or just a premise?** An answer that says the crash data were collected inconsistently attacks the correlation — relevant, but weaker than an answer that accepts the correlation and defeats the cause.
2. **Does it apply to this case?** An answer about roundabouts in general is weaker than one about these towns.
3. **How much of the gap does it close?** "No cause, no effect" closes nearly all of it. A mechanism closes some.

<KeyIdea>
The best weakener accepts every premise and still makes the conclusion unlikely. The best strengthener accepts every premise and makes a rival story impossible.
</KeyIdea>

## Decoys on causal questions

- **Premise boosters.** *"The crash reduction was even larger than reported."* Makes the correlation stronger, not the causation. On Strengthen questions this is the classic trap; on Weaken, the mirror image ("the reduction was smaller") is.
- **Scope shifts.** The argument is about *severity*; the answer is about *frequency*. The argument is about *towns*; the answer is about *highways*.
- **Opposite direction.** Under time, people pick a strengthener on a Weaken question. Translate the stem first; re-read it after choosing.
- **Irrelevant comparisons.** *"Roundabouts are cheaper than traffic lights."* True, interesting, irrelevant.

## The "explain" variant

Resolve/Explain questions give you a *surprising* phenomenon with no conclusion — *crashes fell after roundabouts were built, yet injury claims rose* — and ask for the fact that makes both halves make sense. The right answer is itself a causal story that accounts for the whole discrepancy. Half-right answers explain one side only.

## Worked contrast

> *A clinic that began texting appointment reminders saw no-shows fall by a third. The reminders clearly work.*

- Weaken, alternative cause: *The clinic simultaneously began charging a fee for missed appointments.*
- Weaken, effect without cause: *No-shows fell by a third that year at comparable clinics that sent no reminders.*
- Weaken, sample: *The drop was measured over two weeks following a holiday period with unusually high no-shows.*
- Strengthen, no cause / no effect: *Patients who had opted out of texts showed no change in no-show rates.*
- Strengthen, dose-response: *Patients who received two reminders missed fewer appointments than those who received one.*
- Decoy: *Patients reported finding the reminders helpful.* (Belief, not behavior.)
