## Why answers sound like this

Flaw answers are written abstractly so they can't be matched by topic. *"Treats a correlation between two phenomena as sufficient evidence that one causes the other"* could be the key for coffee, crime, or coral. You need to hear "correlation ≠ causation" without effort.

## The decoder

**Causation**
- *"infers a causal connection from a mere correlation"* → correlation ≠ causation
- *"overlooks the possibility that the two phenomena have a common cause"* → alternative cause
- *"fails to consider that the causal relationship may be the reverse of the one asserted"* → reverse causation

**Sampling & generalization**
- *"draws a general conclusion from a sample that is unlikely to be representative"* → bad sample
- *"generalizes from an atypical case"* → one example

**Composition & division**
- *"infers that a whole has a property because each of its parts does"* → part → whole
- *"assumes that what is true of a group is true of each member"* → whole → part

**Conditional**
- *"treats a condition that is necessary for an outcome as though it were sufficient"* → requirement ≠ guarantee
- *"confuses a sufficient condition with a necessary one"* → reversal
- *"concludes that something will not occur merely because one circumstance that would produce it is absent"* → denying the sufficient

**Strength & scope**
- *"draws a conclusion that is stronger than the premises warrant"* → overreach
- *"treats a relative difference as an absolute one"* → comparison shift
- *"confuses a proportion with an absolute number"* → percent vs count

**Evidence & dialogue**
- *"takes the absence of evidence for a claim as evidence against it"* → absence of evidence
- *"presumes that a claim is false because its proponent has a personal stake"* → ad hominem
- *"appeals to an authority whose expertise lies outside the relevant field"* → irrelevant authority
- *"rejects a position by attacking a distorted version of it"* → straw man

**Language & structure**
- *"relies on an ambiguity in a key term"* → equivocation
- *"presupposes what it sets out to establish"* → circular
- *"presents as the only alternatives two options that are not exhaustive"* → false dilemma
- *"infers what someone believes from what is actually the case"* (or the reverse) → mental state vs reality

<KeyIdea>
Translate each choice into a four-word gap ("correlation to cause," "part to whole," "can to will") *before* judging it. Then ask: did this argument make that exact move?
</KeyIdea>

## Three decoy types

1. **The real flaw, wrong argument.** The choice describes a legitimate error the argument didn't commit. *"Relies on an unrepresentative sample"* when there's no sample. Always check the stimulus for the ingredient the choice names.
2. **Premise attack.** *"Fails to establish that the survey was conducted properly."* Flaw questions grade the inference, not the premises. Unless the argument's own reasoning depends on that, it's a decoy.
3. **The reversed description.** *"Confuses a sufficient condition with a necessary one"* when the argument did the opposite. Both directions get offered; only one matches.

## Two-step check for any Flaw answer

- **Did it?** Point to the words where the argument makes the move the choice describes.
- **Does it matter?** Would fixing that move change whether the conclusion follows?

A choice that passes both is the key. The check below is all Flaw questions; run every choice through the decoder first.
