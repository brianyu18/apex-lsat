## The rule

Once a week, take a timed section under conditions worse than you expect on test day. Your best-case score is not the one you will get; your score under stress is. Train the second one.

## Why

Most practice happens rested, quiet, warmed up, at a time you chose. Test day guarantees none of that. Compare a fresh section with one taken at 9 p.m. after work in `/test/timing`: the slow columns get slower and the "sure" tags less accurate. That gap is what this lesson shrinks.

## The menu

One or two per session:

| Degradation | How |
|---|---|
| Tired | After a full workday, or first thing with no coffee |
| Cold | No warm-up, no review; open `/test/section` and start |
| Noisy | A café, a shared room, a window open on traffic |
| Timer hidden | Hide the clock in the LawHub bar; it returns for the last five minutes |
| Unknown experimental | `/test/pt`, unscored section on random; do not guess which |
| Back-to-back | Two sections, no break, then the real 10-minute break, then a third |
| Wrong order | Start with your weaker section type |

Keep a one-line log of date and condition; the heatmap shows one row per section by date, so the log tells you which rows were degraded.

## The break

The real test gives you ten minutes after section 2. Rehearse it as a procedure: stand, water, bathroom whether needed or not, a few slow breaths, back in the seat with a minute to spare. Do not replay section 2 or estimate a score. The break is for your body; the scoreboard is closed.

## When a section goes badly

The reset is one sentence: *the next question is a new test.* Not the next section, the next question. Trying to recover lost points is how one bad question becomes three. Flag it, pick a survivor, and read the next stimulus as if the section just began.

The PT runner hides which section was unscored until results. Treat every section as scored and every bad one as possibly free.

## Interface habits

Make these automatic in the LawHub shell:

- **Flag** anything deferred or answered on a lean; the bottom navigator's flag list is your return pass.
- **Reset response** for a clean slate instead of a half-remembered first pick.
- **Eliminate** with the strike control, not in your head; struck choices stay struck when you return.
- **Highlight** sparingly in RC: pivots and viewpoint markers, not whole sentences.

## Scratch paper

Conditionals, parallel-structure skeletons, and numbers only; never rewrite a stimulus. One question per block, labeled by number so the return pass can find it. If the sheet fills before the section ends, your diagrams are too large.

## Protocol

<Steps>
1. Choose one degradation and write it in your log before you start.
2. Take the section in `/test/section`, full tags and flags, no concessions.
3. Blind review as normal; the review is never degraded.
4. In `/test/timing`, compare that row and its sure-tag accuracy against a fresh section from the same week.
5. If the drop is more than two questions, repeat that condition next week until it closes.
6. Two weeks out, run one full `/test/pt` cold, at your test-day start time, experimental section unknown.
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Train the worst version of test day on purpose: one degraded section a week, the break as a rehearsed procedure, and a one-sentence reset when a section turns, so nothing on the day happens for the first time.
</KeyIdea>
