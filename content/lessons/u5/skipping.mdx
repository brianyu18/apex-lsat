## The rule

Take every section in two passes. First pass: answer what you can see into; defer what you cannot in ten seconds or less. Return pass: the deferred questions, hardest last. Nothing is left blank.

A skip is not a surrender; it spends that question's time later, when the easy points are banked.

## How many

The program's working policy by goal:

| Goal | First-pass skips per section |
|---|---|
| 175+ | 0 to 2 |
| 170 to 174 | 2 to 4 |
| Below 170 | 3 to 5 |

The logic is arithmetic. A 170 can miss roughly three or four a section; a 175 about one. A target that allows four misses has no reason to pay 150 seconds for a hard question while four cheaper ones wait behind it. A target that allows one needs nearly every question, so you skip only what would become a bleed.

## What to skip

A candidate has at least one of these:

- A dense stimulus: three or more sentences of conditional or causal structure, or two long speakers.
- A type that runs slow for you. Your two slowest types on `/test/timing` are candidates by default.
- A question that needs a diagram (parallel reasoning, a conditional chain, numbers and percentages) when you are already behind a checkpoint.

Not a candidate: a short stimulus, which is almost always cheap, or a type you own. Flaw at 95 percent in 60 seconds does not get skipped for looking wordy.

## The verdicts

`/test/skip-trainer` runs a section where you declare attempt or skip before each question, then scores the decision. "Slow" means more than 1.8 times your median for that section.

| Verdict | What happened | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Clean attempt | Attempted, right, not slow | The system working |
| Costly attempt | Attempted, right, slow | Right, but paid twice; skip this shape next time |
| Time bleed | Attempted, wrong, slow | Long and wrong; costs a later question |
| Good skip | Skipped, right on return | Correct order; point banked |
| Skipped a gimme | Skipped an easy one, lost it | Skips should be hard-looking, not short |

Two neutral verdicts, an honest miss (fast and wrong) and a reasonable skip still missed, score zero and need no fix.

Two bleeds in a section means the 45-second rule is not yet a reflex. More skips than policy means you are abandoning questions you could bank; trust the first read longer. Fewer than policy is fine if you finished comfortably and a problem if the last five were rushed.

## Protocol

<Steps>
1. Set your goal score in the trainer so the policy range is yours.
2. First pass: stem, then stimulus. Structure by 45 seconds: finish it. No structure: pick a survivor, flag, go.
3. Behind at a checkpoint: lower the threshold. The next dense stimulus is a skip on sight.
4. Finish the first pass. Note the clock.
5. Return pass: shortest flagged question first, then your strongest type, then the rest. Reread the stimulus fresh, not your old reasoning.
6. With 30 seconds left, fill every blank with the same letter.
7. Read the verdicts before the answer key; costly attempts and skipped gimmes change next week's plan.
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Skip by policy, not by mood: a budget set by your goal, candidates chosen by density and your own heatmap, and a return pass that pays the deferred price once the cheap points are in the bank.
</KeyIdea>
