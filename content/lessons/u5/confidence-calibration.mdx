## The rule

Every answer you submit in `/test/section` and `/test/pt` carries one of three tags: **sure**, **leaning**, or **guess**. It takes one second and turns a score into a diagnosis.

## What calibration means

You are calibrated when your confidence predicts your accuracy. The program's working targets:

| Tag | Target accuracy | A miss here means |
|---|---|---|
| Sure | 90 percent or higher | A real method error; worth a review card |
| Leaning | 60 to 75 percent | Expected; the second pass should raise it |
| Guess | 25 to 40 percent | Chance plus elimination; fine |

The program computes these from your tagged timed answers; its coaching line speaks once a bucket holds about twenty items. Act on the shape of the three numbers, not the overall score.

## Two bad shapes

**Overconfident:** "sure" accuracy in the 70s or low 80s. You are committing to answers that have not earned it, usually by matching a keyword instead of the prephrase. The fix is procedural: on every "sure" tag, name the trap the runner-up would have been. If you cannot, the tag is "leaning." Review every sure-but-wrong item the same day; those misses do not feel like misses, and they repeat.

**Underconfident:** "leaning" accuracy above 85 percent with half the section tagged leaning. You know more than you trust, and the cost is time: every leaning tag brings an unneeded reread. Commit on the first clean match and move.

Both patterns are invisible without tags; a 22/25 looks identical either way.

## The under-10-second flag

Any answer submitted in under 10 seconds is flagged as a guess, whatever tag you chose; you can un-flag a genuine instant read. Guesses count toward the section score but not toward accuracy or rating updates, and a guess that lands right goes to the inbox as "Guessed right" so you review it as a miss. That keeps buzzer fills from flattering your numbers.

## What blind review adds

Blind review redoes every flagged or uncertain question untimed before the key. Each question then has two answers; the pair is the diagnosis:

| Outcome | Timed, then review | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| right_right | right, right | Known | Nothing |
| wrong_right | wrong, right | Time pressure | Pacing and skip work, not content |
| wrong_wrong | wrong, wrong | Knowledge gap | Back to the type lesson; drill untimed |
| right_wrong | right, wrong | Careless on review, or lucky | If lucky, treat as wrong_wrong |
| blank_* | never reached | Pacing | Count them; they are what bleeds cost |

The ratio of wrong_right to wrong_wrong sets next week. Mostly wrong_right: you have the content and need the clock; sections and the skip trainer. Mostly wrong_wrong: timing work is premature; back to the unit for those types.

<Callout tone="warn" title="Do not look at the key first">
A blind review done after seeing the answers produces right_right everywhere and tells you nothing. The inbox puts the untimed pass before the reveal; keep it that way.
</Callout>

## Protocol

<Steps>
1. Tag every answer before submitting; default to "leaning" when unsure.
2. After the section, blind review every flagged, leaning, and guess item before the key.
3. In the inbox, review sure-but-wrong first, wrong_wrong second.
4. Once a week, check the three accuracies. Sure under 90: the runner-up-trap habit goes on tomorrow's list.
</Steps>

<KeyIdea>
Confidence tags turn a score into a diagnosis: sure-but-wrong is a method error, wrong_right is time pressure, wrong_wrong is a gap, and only one of those is fixed by more timed sections.
</KeyIdea>
