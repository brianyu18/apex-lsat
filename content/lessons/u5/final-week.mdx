## The rule

The final week adds nothing: no new lesson, type, or strategy. Its job is to deliver the score you already have, rested and on schedule.

## The registration decision

Decide before the week starts, from the score range on `/analytics`: the mean of your last five full tests, simulated or imported through `/test/import`, with a margin that narrows to about 1.6 points after five tests.

**Sit the test when the low end of the range is at or above your goal minus 2. Otherwise postpone.**

The low end, not the mean. Goal 170, range 167 to 171: 167 is below 168, so postpone. Test day tends to land near the bottom of the range. Postponing is cheap; a score you must explain is not. A wide blind-review gap is a second reason to wait: the points exist and the clock has not released them.

## Day by day

| Day | Work | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Full `/test/pt` at your test-day start time | Blind review same day; the last test |
| 6 | Review the PT: inbox, outcomes by type, sure-tag accuracy | Nothing timed; rewrite your one-page checklist |
| 5 | Two timed sections, LR and RC | Clean and on pace; nothing heroic |
| 4 | One timed LR section plus `/test/skip-trainer` | Last skip-policy check |
| 3 | One timed section, your weaker of LR and RC | Stop by early afternoon |
| 2 | Ten untimed questions, best types, then stop | Confirm admission and check-in requirements on LSAC's site; set out ID and materials |
| 1 | Nothing hard | A walk, an early night; no sections, no inbox |
| Test | The routine below | |

From day 5 on, every session is shorter than the one before. A bad section this week is a data point about fatigue, not ability; do not add work to compensate.

## Sleep on the test clock

From day 7, sleep and wake at the times test day will demand, check-in margin included. A body that has woken at test time for a week is alert at section 1; one that has done it once is not. Caffeine as usual.

## The day before

Nothing hard means nothing that produces a score: no sections, drills, inbox, or reading about the LSAT online. Reread your checklist once in the morning, then put it away. Eat normally, prepare the room or the route, and go to bed on the test clock whether tired or not.

## Test morning

<Steps>
1. Wake at the rehearsed time. Eat what you ate on day 7.
2. Ten minutes before check-in, two easy LR questions you have seen before, untimed, to warm up.
3. Say the four checkpoints once: nine, fifteen, twenty-four, thirty-three.
4. Confirm room, device, connection, and materials against the day-2 requirements.
5. At the break: stand, water, bathroom, breathe, sit down with a minute to spare. No scoreboard.
6. If a section turns, the next question is a new test.
7. Afterward, write down how it felt before looking anything up; that note starts the next plan, if there is one.
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="What to bring">
Check LSAC's current requirements for admission, identification, and permitted materials, and follow them exactly. Those rules change; this lesson does not restate them.
</Callout>

<KeyIdea>
The final week is a taper: one last full test early, shorter sessions every day after, sleep on the test clock, nothing hard the day before, and a go-or-postpone decision made from the low end of your score range rather than its mean.
</KeyIdea>
