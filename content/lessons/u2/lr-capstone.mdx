## One read

Every LR type in this unit starts the same way: find the conclusion, find what supports it, name the gap. The stem decides what you do with the gap. Map this argument once, then watch six stems reuse the map.

<ArgumentMap

  sentences={[

    { text: "Yet at one mid-sized accounting firm, output per employee rose 12 percent in the two years after the firm went fully remote.", role: "premise", why: "The single piece of evidence; 'yet' turns against the commentators." },
    { text: "During the same period, the firm's employees reported higher job satisfaction in its annual surveys.", role: "premise", why: "A second observation from the same firm, offered alongside the first." },
    { text: "The firm's experience thus shows that remote work can raise output.", role: "sub_conclusion", why: "'Thus' draws it from the two observations; it is then used to reach the final claim." },
    { text: "It is clear, then, that the commentators are wrong: remote work does not reduce productivity.", role: "conclusion", why: "Supported by the sub-conclusion; supports nothing. Note the scope: one firm became a claim about remote work generally." }
  ]}

**The gap, in plain words:** one firm's two-year rise is treated as proof about remote work in general, and the rise is attributed to remote work with no other cause considered.

## Six stems, one gap

| Stem | Operation on the gap | What the credited answer does |
|---|---|---|
| Flaw | **name** it | "generalizes from a single case" or "overlooks other explanations for the increase" |
| Necessary Assumption | **protect** it | "the firm is not atypical" / "nothing else explains the rise." Negate: the argument collapses |
| Weaken | **attack** it | a fact: the firm cut its lowest-performing third of staff the year it went remote |
| Strengthen | **patch** it | a fact: comparable firms that stayed in-office saw flat output over the same two years |
| Most Strongly Supported | **stay below** it | "at least one firm's output rose after it went remote." Far weaker than the author's own conclusion, which is the point |
| Parallel Flaw | **copy** it | "One patient improved after taking the herb, so the herb can help; so the herb is not ineffective." Single case, sub-conclusion, overreach |

Nothing changed but the operation. Argument Part and Main Conclusion use the same map: sentence 4 is the sub-conclusion, sentence 5 the main conclusion.

## Triage: where the minutes go

Twenty-five questions in 35 minutes is 84 seconds each. Questions get harder toward the end, so bank time early and spend it late. Type is a rough guide to cost:

| Cost | Types | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fast (under a minute) | Main Conclusion, Argument Part, Point at Issue, short MSS/MBT, Explain | structural read, short choices |
| Medium (one to two minutes) | Flaw, Weaken, Strengthen, NA, SA, Principle, Method, Fill in the Blank, Evaluate | need a prephrase; five choices to test |
| Slow (two minutes plus) | Parallel Reasoning, Parallel Flaw, long Rule Application, abstract Method | five arguments to diagram, not one |

The rules:

1. **Work in order, defer by cost.** First pass: skip Parallel questions with long choices, and any question you have read twice without a prephrase. Mark it; move on.
2. **Never skip on type alone.** A short Parallel with simple conditionals is a 60-second question. Glance at the choices' length first.
3. **Return with a plan.** Restart from your prephrase, not from choice A.
4. **The last five minutes go to deferred questions,** not to rereading answers you already chose.

## The level-4 section

Hard sections hide the cost. A level-4 Flaw with five fully abstract choices costs more than an easy Parallel. Judge by the choices, not the stem: long, abstract, twisted choices mean slow, whatever the type.

<KeyIdea>
The read never changes: conclusion, support, gap. The stem only picks the operation to run on the gap: name it (Flaw), protect it (NA), attack it (Weaken), patch it (Strengthen), stay below it (MSS), or copy it (Parallel Flaw). One read, every stem.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices, on every question

1. Conclusion, support, gap, in that order, in your own words.
2. Say which operation the stem asks for.
3. Prephrase in the form that operation requires: a description, a negatable statement, a fact, a modest claim, or a skeleton.
