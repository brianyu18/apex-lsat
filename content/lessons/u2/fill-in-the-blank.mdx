## The stem and the task

The stimulus ends with `____.` and the stem reads *"Which one of the following most logically completes the argument?"*

This is not its own kind of reasoning. It is one of two familiar tasks in disguise, and the connective right before the blank tells you which:

| Word before the blank | The blank is | The task is |
|---|---|---|
| therefore, so, thus, it follows that, hence | the conclusion | Most Strongly Supported: what do these premises establish? |
| since, because, for, given that | a premise | Sufficient Assumption: what makes the stated conclusion follow? |

Read that word first. Everything else depends on it.

## Premise blank: run the SA method

<Example label="Premise blank">
*"The lizards on the island are genetically identical to a mainland population, yet the channel between them has strong currents and no lizard has ever been observed swimming it. The species therefore cannot have reached the island by swimming, since ____."* The blank must rule out swimming: *no animal can cross a channel with such currents by swimming*. Bridge the conclusion's new idea back to the facts, exactly as you would for a Sufficient Assumption.
</Example>

## What the right answer looks like

- **Conclusion blank**: cautious, uses the stimulus's own terms, follows from all the premises, descriptive if the premises are descriptive.
- **Premise blank**: the missing link that, added to the stated premises, makes the conclusion follow; usually a general statement connecting a premise term to the conclusion term.
- In both: it obeys the connective. "Nevertheless, ____" demands a claim that cuts against the concession before it; "for example, ____" demands an instance.

## The level-4 version

- **The premise blank in disguise.** The blank follows "for" or "because," and the choices all look like conclusions. The credited one is a bridging conditional, and the MSS-style modest claim is the trap.
- **A blank after a concession.** "Admittedly X. Nevertheless, ____." The completion must be the conclusion the concession tried to block, in the author's force.
- **A blank inside a dialogue.** The second speaker's reply ends in the blank; complete it as a response to the first speaker's point, not as a free-standing claim.

<KeyIdea>
The word before the blank tells you the task. After *therefore*, *so*, *it follows that*: supply the modest conclusion the premises support. After *since*, *because*, *for*: supply the premise that makes the stated conclusion follow.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Read the connective; label the blank conclusion or premise.
2. Conclusion blank: prephrase the weakest claim all the facts force. Premise blank: name the term in the conclusion that needs a bridge.
3. Note whether the premises are descriptive or normative; the completion must match.
