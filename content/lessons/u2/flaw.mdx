## The stem and the task

> *The reasoning in the argument is most vulnerable to criticism on the grounds that it…* / *The argument is flawed in that it…* / *Which one of the following most accurately describes a flaw in the argument's reasoning?*

Flaw is the most common LR type — about four per section. You are not adding a fact and not disproving the conclusion. You are **describing the gap**, in the abstract vocabulary the test uses for gaps. Every right answer passes a two-part test: (1) the argument actually does this, and (2) doing this is why the premises fail to establish the conclusion.

## What the right answer looks like

- A description, usually abstract: *takes for granted that…*, *fails to consider the possibility that…*, *infers from … that …*, *treats … as if it were …*.
- Translated back into the stimulus, every noun in it maps onto something that is actually there.
- It names the **gap** — not a premise, not the conclusion. "Presumes that most residents oppose the ordinance" restates the conclusion; restating is not a flaw.
- A *fails to consider that X* answer is right only when X, if true, would damage the argument. The argument fails to consider a thousand things; one of them matters.
- Force is calibrated. "Takes for granted that letter-writers are representative" matches what the argument did; "assumes every letter-writer is a resident" is more than it did.

## The level-4 variant

Hard Flaw questions do two things. First, the gap hides in a near-synonym shift — *approved of* becomes *benefited from*, *reported* becomes *experienced* — so the stimulus reads as valid until you line up the conclusion's nouns against the premises'. Second, the right answer is phrased one level more abstract than your prephrase: not "assumes the sample is representative" but *draws a conclusion about a population from a group whose members selected themselves into it*. The best trap is a textbook flaw name (*correlation and causation*, *necessary and sufficient*) that this argument doesn't commit.

Run two passes. First, kill every answer that describes something the argument doesn't do. Second, among the survivors, pick the one whose "something" is the reason the conclusion doesn't follow.

<KeyIdea>
A Flaw answer is a true description of the argument that is also an explanation of why it fails. Translate each abstract choice back into the stimulus's own nouns; if a noun has no referent, or the described move isn't the gap, it's out.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Conclusion in your own words; premises in one line.
2. The gap in one sentence: "It assumes ___."
3. The gap's abstract name, if it has one: sampling, causation, conditional, term shift, part–whole.
