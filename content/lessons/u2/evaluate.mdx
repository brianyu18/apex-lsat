## The stem and the task

*"Which one of the following would be most useful to know in evaluating the argument?"*

The choices are questions, or "whether" clauses. The credited one targets the argument's assumption: answer it one way and the argument gets stronger; answer it the other way and it gets weaker. Call this the **both-ways test**. A question whose every answer leaves the argument where it was is noise, however interesting.

## What the right answer looks like

- A **question** aimed at the assumption, not at a premise's truth and not at whether the conclusion matters.
- Its answers **swing both ways**; the bigger the swing, the better the choice.
- It respects the stimulus's **exact terms** and often concerns the hidden **baseline**: compared with what, over what period, among whom.
- It is not satisfied by information already in the stimulus.

## The level-4 version

- **Abstract or quantitative phrasing.** "The proportion of the chain's flu-shot customers who had received a flu shot the previous year." You must compute: high proportion → repeat customers, weak evidence of new vaccinations; low → new people. The swing is there, but you have to work it out.
- **Two assumptions, two candidates.** One choice tests the term shift, another tests an alternative cause. Pick the one whose answers swing the conclusion further, usually the one closest to the conclusion's own terms.
- **EXCEPT and "least useful."** Four questions bear on the argument; the odd one fails the both-ways test. Grid all five.

<KeyIdea>
Evaluate is Weaken and Strengthen in one question. Find the assumption; the credited choice is the question whose Yes and No answers push the conclusion in opposite directions. If both answers leave the argument where it was, the question is noise.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Write the conclusion and the evidence in their exact terms.
2. Name the top two assumptions (term shift, alternative cause).
3. Phrase each as a question and plan to run both-ways on every choice.
