## The stem and the task

*"The argument proceeds by"* or *"Which one of the following most accurately describes the technique of reasoning used?"* In dialogues: *"[B] responds to [A] by."*

This is Flaw without the error. You describe how the argument gets from premises to conclusion, in the abstract. The discipline is the **referent test**: each noun phrase in a choice ("a general principle," "a counterexample," "an analogy") must point at something specific in the stimulus. One noun with no referent and the choice is describing an argument that isn't there.

## The decoder: common method language

| Answer says | Concretely, the argument… |
|---|---|
| "appeals to a general principle" | states a rule and applies it to the case |
| "offers a counterexample" | gives one case that breaks a universal claim |
| "argues by analogy" | treats a similar case as evidence for this one |
| "undermines a premise" | denies a reason the other side gave |
| "questions the relevance of evidence" | accepts the evidence is true; denies it bears on the conclusion |
| "shows a claim leads to an unacceptable consequence" | reductio: grants the claim, derives something absurd |
| "distinguishes two senses of a term" | points out an ambiguity the other side relied on |
| "rules out alternative explanations" | eliminates rival causes to support one |

## What the right answer looks like

- Every noun has a **referent**; every verb matches the **strength** of what was done (*questions* vs *refutes* vs *proves*).
- It describes the **move**, with no content words from the topic and no evaluation of whether it worked.
- In a dialogue, it describes the responder's move **relative to** the first speaker's argument.

## The level-4 version

- **Fully abstract twins.** Right answer: "concluding that a generalization is unlikely to hold in a particular case because it has failed in relevantly similar cases." Best trap: "…because it has failed in *some* cases." One adjective, *relevantly similar*, carries the referent (comparable cities).
- **The responder shifts the issue.** The second speaker answers a different question than was asked, and the credited answer says so: "responds to a claim about X by addressing a claim about Y."
- **Method one level up.** Your prephrase is at the level of "cites three cities"; the answer is at the level of "appeals to inductive evidence." Practice translating up a level without losing the referent.

<KeyIdea>
Method answers are abstract summaries. Prove each abstract noun against a concrete piece of the text and each verb against the actual strength of the move. A noun without a referent means the choice describes a different argument.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Name the conclusion and, in a dialogue, the claim being answered.
2. Write the move as one abstract sentence.
3. List the referent for each noun in your sentence; carry that list into the choices.
