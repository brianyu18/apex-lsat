## The stem and the task

*"Which one of the following most accurately expresses the main conclusion of the argument?"*

The answer is a paraphrase of the author's final claim, not a quotation. The test hides that claim: after an opposing view, in the middle of the paragraph, behind a decoy "therefore" that marks a sub-conclusion. The method from Unit 1 still governs: the main conclusion is the claim everything else supports and that supports nothing.

## What the right answer looks like

- A **paraphrase** with the same **force**: if the author said *probably*, the answer says *likely*; if the author said *not necessarily*, the answer does not say *never*.
- Same **scope**: the author's claim about *this* canal does not become a claim about canals.
- The author's **own** claim, stripped of connectives (*but*, *in fact*, *clearly*).
- Often an unmarked sentence that follows a *however*, or the first sentence, which the rest then defends.

## The level-4 version

- **The decoy "therefore."** A mid-stimulus "thus" marks a sub-conclusion, and an unmarked later sentence is the main point. The because-test exposes it: the "thus" sentence supports the later one, not the reverse.
- **Conclusion first, then defended.** "The proposal will not save money." followed by three sentences of support. No indicator anywhere; the paragraph's shape is the indicator.
- **A response as conclusion.** "This objection fails" or a rhetorical question ("Why should we assume…?") is the conclusion. Answers must translate it into a declarative claim.
- **Hedged conclusions.** "Not necessarily," "need not," "there is little reason to think" are the author's force. The extreme answer is always waiting.

<KeyIdea>
The main conclusion is the one claim that is supported and supports nothing further. Indicator words nominate candidates; the because-test elects. Then match force and scope, not wording.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Strike every attributed view ("some say," "critics argue").
2. Arrow each remaining claim; the one with no outgoing arrow is the conclusion.
3. Note its force word (*probably*, *not necessarily*, *must*) so you can check it against the choices.
