## The stems and the tasks

**Conform** (specific → general): *"Which one of the following principles, if valid, most helps to justify the reasoning above?"* and the plain form, *"The situation described above most closely conforms to which one of the following principles?"* The stimulus is a case or a short argument; the answer is the general rule it instantiates.

**Apply** (general → specific): *"Which one of the following judgments conforms most closely to the principle stated above?"* The stimulus is the rule; the answer is a case in which every condition of the rule is met and the rule's own verdict is reached.

Both are rule-matching. The direction tells you which side is fixed.

## Conform: the case must fill the rule, not the reverse

<Example label="Conform">
A hospital let a nurse who had reported a safety violation transfer departments at her request, although her department was short-staffed. Credited principle: *An institution should accommodate reasonable requests from employees who report safety violations, even at some cost to the institution.* Every element (report, reasonable request, cost, accommodation) has a referent in the case.
</Example>

The principle may be broader than the case, but it may not contain an element the case lacks, and it may not reach a different verdict.

## What the right answer looks like

- **Apply**: every slot filled, including timing and exceptions; the verdict is the rule's own verdict, in the rule's strength (*should*, *may*, *required*).
- **Conform**: a general statement whose every element appears in the case, reaching the case's actual outcome.
- Neither direction ever adds a judgment the rule does not make. A case the trigger misses gets no verdict.

## The level-4 version

- **Apply, exception as the answer.** The credited choice is the case where the exception fires, so the verdict is *may evaluate*, not *should decline*. Students who prephrased only the main verdict skip it.
- **Two rules, one case.** The stimulus gives two principles; the choice must satisfy both. The trap satisfies one.
- **Conform in the abstract.** The principle is written as "an agent who … incurs an obligation to …" or in the contrapositive. Translate to slots before matching.

<KeyIdea>
Principles are conditionals with slots: trigger, qualifiers, exception, verdict. Conform: the case fills every slot of the rule. Apply: the choice fills every slot and reaches exactly the rule's verdict. Where the trigger is unmet the rule is silent, and silence is not a judgment.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Write the rule as slots: trigger (with every qualifier), exception, verdict.
2. Note what the rule is silent about.
3. Check choices slot by slot; stop at the first empty one.
