## The stem and the task

*"Which one of the following principles, if valid, most helps to justify the [person]'s reasoning?"* or *"…most helps to justify the conclusion drawn?"*

This is Sufficient Assumption wearing a general rule. The stimulus moves from facts to a conclusion, usually a judgment: *irresponsible*, *should*, *justified*. Nothing in the premises carries that judgment word. The credited principle is a conditional that fires on the facts given and lands on the conclusion. "Most helps" means it need not be airtight, but the winner nearly always is.

## What the right answer looks like

- A **conditional**, general in form: premise-situation on the IF side, conclusion on the THEN side.
- The IF side may be **broader** than the facts (a broad rule still fires) but never **narrower** or **stronger** (it then fails to fire).
- A normative conclusion needs a normative THEN side. *Should*, *ought*, *wrong*, *justified* must appear there.
- Direction is not negotiable. The converse justifies nothing.

## The level-4 version

- **Contrapositive phrasing.** "Unless a body's expenditures are supported by its own analysis, it cannot be said to act responsibly." Translate before judging: not supported → not responsible. Same rule, hidden.
- **Two gaps, one rule.** A long stimulus has a sub-conclusion; only one of the two inferences needs bridging. The trap bridges the other.
- **"Most helps" with no perfect fit.** When no rule closes the gap completely, prefer the rule whose trigger is fully met over a stronger rule whose trigger is not. A rule that doesn't fire helps zero.

<KeyIdea>
A justifying principle is a general conditional: premise-situation on the IF side, conclusion on the THEN side. Check that the trigger fires on the facts exactly as stated, then check that it lands on the conclusion's own judgment word.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Circle the conclusion's new term (the judgment word).
2. Write your IF → THEN rule in the stimulus's vocabulary.
3. Plan to test trigger first, landing second, on every choice.
