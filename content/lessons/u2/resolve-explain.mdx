## The stem and the task

*"Which one of the following, if true, most helps to explain the discrepancy described above?"* or *"…most helps to resolve the apparent paradox?"*

There is no argument here. The stimulus gives two facts that sit uneasily together: you expected X, you got Y. Your job is not to decide which fact is wrong. Both are true. You supply a third fact that makes the pairing unsurprising.

## What the right answer looks like

- It **adds** a fact; it never contradicts either half. "The injury figures were miscounted" is not an explanation, it's a denial.
- It touches **both** halves, or supplies a mechanism that connects them.
- It respects the exact terms: *reported* injuries, the *number* of injuries, *this* city, *this* period. A fact about a different group or a static fact about the city cannot explain a change.
- New information is fine. "Out of scope" is not a kill on Explain; "doesn't connect" is.

## The level-4 version

Three ways the hard version differs:

1. **The expectation is unstated.** The stimulus reports one result with "surprisingly" or "nevertheless," and you must reconstruct what was expected before you can see what needs explaining.
2. **The credited answer looks like it deepens the paradox.** "The lanes made cycling feel safer" sounds like it should reduce injuries, until you see it draws out cautious new riders. Read each choice to its consequence, not its first impression.
3. **EXCEPT stems.** "Each of the following, if true, helps to explain… EXCEPT." Four choices explain; the odd one is irrelevant or touches one half. Grid them: explains / doesn't.

<KeyIdea>
An Explain answer is a third fact that lets both halves stand and makes their pairing expected. If you must deny a half, or the fact touches only one half, it is wrong, however plausible it sounds.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Write the tension: "expected ___, got ___."
2. Underline the exact terms of each half (count vs rate, reported vs actual, which group, which period).
3. Prephrase two or three *shapes* of explanation, not one story.
