## The stem and the task

> *Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?* / *…most supports the [person]'s claim?*

Same gap as Weaken, opposite direction. You add a true fact that raises the support the premises give the conclusion. You do not need to prove it; a modest fact that lands on the gap beats a sweeping fact that lands beside it. The best answer closes the biggest hole.

## What the right answer looks like

- A new fact that **closes a specific hole**: removes an alternative explanation, rules out reverse causation, shows the sample is representative, or supplies the bridge between the premise's term and the conclusion's.
- Causal conclusions: the strongest strengtheners show the cause varying with the effect under control — no cause, no effect; cause introduced, effect appears — or eliminate a named rival.
- Non-causal conclusions: a fact tying X to Y ("at this school, test scores closely track independent measures of understanding").
- Stating a necessary assumption *is* strengthening. If the argument needs "the ledgers are complete," a choice confirming it helps.
- Restating the premise never helps. The premise was already granted.

## The level-4 variant

At level 4 the strengthener is often a principle or a general conditional you must apply yourself: *Habits formed in childhood are rarely abandoned when their health effects become known* strengthens an argument you would never have phrased that way. Or it rules out a rival you hadn't listed — so prephrase the *category* ("eliminates another cause") rather than one fact. Between two helpers, "most strengthens" prefers the one that addresses the conclusion's actual scope: if the conclusion says *helps prevent*, a choice establishing *is the main preventive factor* is more than needed but still strengthens; a choice restating the correlation does nothing.

Watch for the choice that only makes a premise more credible. Premises are already true; supporting them leaves the gap untouched.

<KeyIdea>
Find the gap, name the kind of fact that would close it, then pick the choice that closes the most of it. A right answer on Strengthen is the denial of a right answer on Weaken.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Conclusion with its force word (*helps*, *is the cause*, *should*).
2. The biggest hole: alternative cause, reverse cause, unrepresentative data, or term shift.
3. The fact that would plug it: "It would help if ___."
