## The stem and the task

> *Which one of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the argument?* / *…most calls into question…*

"If true" means you don't get to doubt the choice. "Weakens the argument" means you lower the support the premises give the conclusion. You are not asked to prove the conclusion false, and you cannot attack a premise — premises are given. Every weakener works by widening the gap between evidence and conclusion.

## What the right answer looks like

- A **new fact**, not a critique of reasoning. "The author overlooks other explanations" is a Flaw answer; on Weaken, the choice *is* the other explanation.
- It leaves every premise standing and makes the conclusion less likely anyway.
- Causal conclusions: an alternative cause, a reversed cause, the effect occurring without the cause (or the cause without the effect), or a problem with how the data were gathered.
- Non-causal conclusions: find the term that shifts between premise and conclusion and show the two come apart — the premise is about X, the conclusion about Y, and the choice shows X without Y.
- Force can be modest. *Some* or *often* is enough if it lands on the gap; *always* is useless if it lands beside it.

## The level-4 variant

Hard Weaken questions hide the conclusion's scope. A proposal argument ("this plan will achieve its goal") is weakened by a fact about *the goal*, not about the plan's cost or popularity. A comparative conclusion ("X is more effective than Y") is weakened by a fact about Y as readily as one about X. And the right answer may be stated as a general principle rather than a fact about the case — *Districts that publicize a crime-prevention measure typically see reporting drop for a year* — which you must apply yourself. When two choices both hurt, "most seriously" picks the one aimed at the main conclusion rather than a sub-point, and the one that removes support rather than adding noise.

<KeyIdea>
Prephrase the gap, then prephrase the kind of fact that would exploit it. Read every choice asking one question: with this added and the premises untouched, is the conclusion less likely than it was?
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Conclusion, exactly — including its scope words (*most*, *will*, *more than*).
2. Causal or not? If causal, list the alternatives in three words each.
3. The sentence that would hurt most: "It would hurt if ___."
