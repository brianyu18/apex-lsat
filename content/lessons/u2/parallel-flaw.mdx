## The stem and the task

*"The flawed reasoning in which one of the following is most similar to that in the argument above?"*

Parallel Reasoning plus Flaw. The stem tells you the original is broken, so your first job is to name the break using the flaw families from Unit 1. Your second job is the ordinary Parallel work: match the conclusion type and premise structure. Both filters are needed because the test will give you one choice with the same flaw in a different structure and one with the same structure reasoning validly.

## What the right answer looks like

- Same **flaw family**, committed in the same **direction** (affirming the consequent is not denying the antecedent, even though both are conditional errors).
- Same **conclusion type**: a hedged prediction matches a hedged prediction.
- Same **premise structure**: one universal plus one particular, not two universals.
- Not valid. Ever. A choice that happens to reason correctly is out before you check anything else.

## The level-4 version

- **Subtle families.** Equivocation, part/whole, percentage versus number. The structure is long and the flaw is a single shifted word. Name the shift precisely ("rate" became "count") so you can look for the same shift.
- **Same family, different mechanism.** Two choices both commit a sampling flaw; one from a biased sample, one from a tiny sample. Match the mechanism the original used.
- **Sub-conclusions.** The original reaches an intermediate claim and then overreaches. The match must have the same two-step shape, with the flaw at the same step.
- **Conclusion stronger than premises.** "Some" became "all," or "can" became "will." The kill word in the trap is often the quantifier alone.

<KeyIdea>
Parallel Flaw is two filters in order: name the flaw family and its direction, then match the skeleton (conclusion type, premise structure). A valid choice is out no matter how similar it looks; a same-flaw choice with a different conclusion type loses to one that matches both.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Diagram the original and say the flaw in family terms.
2. Type the conclusion on four axes.
3. Plan the order: kill valid choices and wrong families first, then compare structure.
