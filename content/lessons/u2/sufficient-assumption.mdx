## The stem and the task

> *The conclusion follows logically if which one of the following is assumed?* / *Which one of the following, if assumed, enables the conclusion to be properly drawn?*

Find a statement that, added to the premises, makes the conclusion **follow with certainty**. Not probable — guaranteed. This is a validity task, so treat the argument as formal logic: premises are terms linked by arrows, the conclusion introduces a new term, and the answer is the arrow from the premises' last term to that new term.

## What the right answer looks like

- A **conditional or universal** linking a premise term to the conclusion term: *any*, *all*, *every*, *no*, *whenever* — or its contrapositive.
- At least as strong as the gap. Stronger is fine; overkill still guarantees. A choice broader than the argument is not "out of scope" if it covers the case.
- Plug-in test: premises + choice → conclusion, with nothing left over. If a second assumption is still needed, it isn't sufficient.
- In quantified arguments, *some* conclusions need *all* bridges. *Some A are B* plus *all B are C* yields *some A are C*; plus *most B are C* yields nothing.
- Direction matters. The arrow must run from what the premises establish to what the conclusion claims.

## Sufficient versus Necessary

| | Sufficient | Necessary |
|---|---|---|
| Test | add it: is the conclusion forced? | negate it: is the argument dead? |
| Strength | strong, often broad | weak, often negative |
| Trap for the other type | the required plank (too weak) | the full bridge (too strong) |

Many wrong answers on one type are right answers on the other.

## The level-4 variant

Level-4 SA moves the gap. The missing link may sit between two premises rather than at the conclusion, so chain first and look for the term that never connects. The correct answer is often a contrapositive with negated terms — *Nothing that fails to improve productivity should be built* — or so broad it sounds off-topic (*Any measure that benefits thousands of residents should be adopted*). Quantifier SAs require *all* in the bridge even when the conclusion is *some*. The best trap is a necessary assumption dressed as a bridge: it uses *can*, *may*, *some*, or *most*, and the plug-in test exposes it because the conclusion still doesn't follow.

<KeyIdea>
Formalize, chain, and find the orphan term in the conclusion. The answer is the arrow from the chain's end to that term — strong enough that, once added, no other outcome is possible.
</KeyIdea>

## Before you read the choices

1. Notation: each premise as an arrow; the conclusion as an arrow.
2. Chain the premises; name the conclusion term the chain never reaches.
3. Write the bridge — last premise term → conclusion term — and its contrapositive.
