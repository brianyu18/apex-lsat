## The pattern

A general rule (principle, policy, statute, definition) is applied to a particular case. The application is sound only if the case satisfies the rule's trigger, falls inside the rule's domain, escapes its exceptions, and the conclusion claims exactly the rule's output.

It is the whole of **Rule Application** and **Principle (Conform)**, half of **Principle (Justify)**, and a frequent structure in **Must Be True**, **Must Be False**, **Flaw**, **Sufficient Assumption**, and **Parallel Reasoning**.

## How to recognize it

A rule has four parts. Mark each in the stimulus:

1. **Domain.** Who the rule covers: *employees, students, members, property owners*.
2. **Trigger.** The sufficient condition, often with several conjuncts: *who work more than 40 hours in a week*.
3. **Exception.** *Unless, except when, provided that, other than* — a clause that switches the rule off.
4. **Output.** The consequence, with its modality: *must be paid, may remove, is eligible, is not permitted*.

Then four checks on the case: Is it in the domain? Does it meet every conjunct of the trigger? Is the exception ruled out? Does the conclusion match the output's modality, without upgrading *may* to *must*, or *eligible* to *entitled*?

One more: a rule states a sufficient condition. A case that fails the trigger is not thereby outside the output. "Course completers are eligible" says nothing about non-completers.

## How it is exploited

| Question | What the key does | Abstract language |
|---|---|---|
| Rule Application | Picks the case that satisfies every conjunct, clears the exception, and ends with the rule's exact output | Traps: the output without the trigger (reversal); the trigger with a stronger output; a case sitting in the exception |
| Principle (Conform) | Abstracts the case's trigger and verdict first, then finds the rule with both | A rule with the right verdict and the wrong trigger is the standard trap |
| Principle (Justify) | Finds the rule whose trigger is the stated premises and whose output is the stated conclusion | Reversed rules and "only if" rules are traps |
| MBT / MBF | Chains the case through the rule and stops | What must follow is the output, nothing about cases that miss the trigger |
| Flaw | Names the failed check | "applies a rule to a case it does not cover" / "treats a sufficient condition as necessary" / "overlooks an exception the rule provides" |
| NA / SA | NA: "The case does not fall under the exception"; SA: the full trigger, asserted of the case | |

## The level-4 variant

The rule is written in prose with its trigger split across two sentences, or with a nested exception: "unless the student has an excuse, in which case the penalty applies only if the paper is more than a week late." The case description then includes a detail that activates an exception without naming it ("Chen's note from the clinic").

Modality is the other hard axis. *Need not* versus *must not*; *ought not be condemned* versus *ought to be praised*; *is permitted* versus *is required*. In **Principle (Conform)**, two answer choices routinely differ only in whether the output is permissive or obligatory, and only one matches the case's verdict.

<KeyIdea>
Treat every rule as a machine: domain, trigger, exception, output. Run the case through mechanically. Every input condition present, every exception absent, the conclusion no stronger than the output, and nothing concluded about cases that miss the trigger. Whichever step the argument skipped is the answer.
</KeyIdea>
