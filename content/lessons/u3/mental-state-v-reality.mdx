## The pattern

A premise about a mind (belief, intention, claim, perception, expectation) is used to support a conclusion about the world, or a fact about the world is used to conclude what someone must believe or intend. Neither direction is free.

It appears in **Flaw**, **Weaken**, **Strengthen**, **Necessary Assumption**, **Resolve/Explain**, **Point at Issue**, and as a trap in **Most Strongly Supported**.

## How to recognize it

Verbs of mind seal their contents in a box: *believes, thinks, claims, says, reports, suspects, expects, intends, wants, hopes, feels, perceives, is confident, considers*. Inside the box, the content can be true or false. An argument that pulls the content out, treating "residents believe the water is clean" as "the water is clean," has crossed the line.

Three verbs open the box legitimately: **know, realize, be aware that** (and *recognize, discover*). These are factive: you cannot know what is false. "Most clients know their data is stored overseas" entails that it is. The test uses both kinds and expects you to tell them apart.

Intent versus outcome is the same pattern with different verbs: *intended, tried, aimed, was designed to* describe a goal, not a result.

## How it is exploited

| Question | What the key does | Abstract language |
|---|---|---|
| Flaw | Names the crossing | "treats a claim about what people believe as if it were a claim about what is the case" / "confuses the intended effect of an action with its actual effect" / "takes the fact that a claim was made as evidence that it is true" |
| Weaken | Separates mind from world | "Those surveyed had no way of checking…" / "The source stood to gain from…" |
| Strengthen | Ties them together | "Residents' estimates have historically matched measured values" |
| Necessary Assumption | Asserts the match | "People's reports of X accurately reflect X" / "The stated goal was the actual goal" |
| Resolve / Explain | Explains a gap between saying and doing | "People report one preference and act on another" |
| MSS / MBT | Keeps the wrapper | "Some residents believe…" is supported; "the water is…" is the trap |

## The level-4 variant

The mental verb hides inside a noun: *reputation, perceived risk, consumer confidence, stated preference, reported income, estimated cost, apparent, alleged, nominal, official*. The argument then reasons about the noun as if it were the thing. "The region's perceived crime rate fell, so residents are safer." Nothing happened to crime.

Two-layer versions test whether you notice which box you are in: "Experts predicted the collapse, and it occurred, so the experts understood its causes." Predictive success is a fact; understanding is a mental state inferred from it. And the reverse crossing appears in Flaw stimuli as "the data was public, so investors must have known."

<KeyIdea>
Every verb of mind is a sealed box. Content comes out of the box, or a fact goes in, only with a premise that licenses it, except for the factive verbs (know, realize, be aware), which open the box for free. Find the box; check which way the argument is reaching through it.
</KeyIdea>
