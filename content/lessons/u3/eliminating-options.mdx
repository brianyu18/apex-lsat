## The pattern

An argument establishes one option (cause, explanation, culprit, course of action) by ruling out the others. The inference is valid only if the list of options was exhaustive and every elimination was earned.

It appears in **Flaw**, **Weaken**, **Strengthen**, **Necessary Assumption**, **Sufficient Assumption**, **Parallel Flaw**, and underlies many **Resolve/Explain** stimuli, where the puzzle exists because the obvious options have been ruled out.

## How to recognize it

Structural tells: *either… or, the only other possibility, since it was not X it must be Y, the only explanation, no other explanation has been proposed, nothing else could account for, the remaining candidate, must have been*. The conclusion is often marked with *must*, because elimination feels deductive.

Two failure points, and the test prefers the first:

1. **The list.** Two options presented as the only two (false dilemma); three causes considered when a fourth exists; "the only explanation anyone has offered" treated as the only explanation there is.
2. **The eliminations.** An option dismissed on thin grounds, often by a lack-of-evidence move ("there is no sign that the price changed").

A valid version exists. When the stimulus stipulates exhaustiveness ("every entry was classified as either fiction or nonfiction"), elimination is airtight. Check whether the list was given or assumed.

## How it is exploited

| Question | What the key does | Abstract language |
|---|---|---|
| Flaw | Names the incomplete list or the lazy elimination | "fails to consider other possible explanations" / "treats two alternatives as exhaustive when they may not be" / "infers that an explanation is correct merely because others have been rejected" |
| Weaken | Supplies the unlisted option, or rehabilitates an eliminated one | "A major distributor dropped the product last quarter" / "The price did change for online buyers" |
| Strengthen | Closes the list or confirms an elimination | "No factor other than quality, price, and advertising affects the product's sales" |
| Necessary Assumption | The exhaustiveness premise, weak form | "There is no cause of the decline other than those considered" |
| Sufficient Assumption | The exhaustive disjunction, outright | "The decline was due to quality, price, or the competitor's campaign" |
| Resolve / Explain | Adds the option the stimulus overlooked | The puzzle is a dilemma; the answer is the third horn |

## The level-4 variant

The options are never labeled as options. Across four sentences the argument sets aside the weather, the staff, and the equipment one at a time, then concludes about the supplier. You reconstruct the list yourself, and the question tests whether you noticed it was built by the arguer.

Second form: the options are not exclusive. "The decline was caused by the price increase, not by the recession" assumes one cause when both could operate. Elimination of a rival cause is not required when causes can share the work.

Trap: a Weaken answer that offers an alternative the stimulus already eliminated. It feels like the right move and attacks nothing.

<KeyIdea>
When a conclusion survives by elimination, ask two questions in order. Was the list complete? Was each elimination earned? The stimulus took one of those on faith, almost always the first, and the correct answer is about that one.
</KeyIdea>
