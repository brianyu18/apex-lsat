## Three findings that should shape every session

1. **Practice testing and spaced practice are the two most effective study techniques measured** — far ahead of rereading and highlighting. The drill *is* the lesson; re-drilling a miss days later is where the learning compounds.
2. **More full practice tests correlate with higher scores, but only up to a point.** LSAC's own data on 12,000 students shows a steady gain through about ten full tests. Beyond that, raw volume stalls if you don't review.
3. **Most plateaus are review failures, not content failures.** People stuck at 165 are usually taking tests and skimming explanations. Breakthroughs come after weeks of flat scores when the *cause* of the misses finally changes.

<KeyIdea>
Accuracy first, then speed. Timing pressure on a method that isn't reliable yet teaches you to guess faster.
</KeyIdea>

## The shape of a study day

One focused block beats two distracted ones. A typical day on your plan:

<Steps>
1. **Review inbox first (10–15 min).** Tag yesterday's misses while they're fresh. Re-drill anything due.
2. **Lesson or type drill (25–40 min).** In the Foundations and LR phases this is a lesson + check or an untimed pair drill. In later phases it's a timed section.
3. **Blind review (whatever is left).** After any timed work, redo every flagged question untimed *before* you look at the answers. Record both scores.
</Steps>

Six study days a week is the recommended default. Consistency beats volume: the plan counts a day as done if you answered at least one question, and your streak only counts days against the goal you set.

## When to go timed

Not yet. You'll move from untimed to timed when, for a question type, your untimed accuracy on level 1–3 questions is above ~85% across the last 20. Before that, a timer measures reading speed, not reasoning. Once timed, the gap between your timed score and your blind-review score tells you what to fix:

- **Big gap (8+ points):** you know it but can't execute under time → pacing, skipping, confidence.
- **Small or no gap:** the ceiling is content → back to untimed drilling on the weakest types.

## Breaking a plateau

If three timed sections in a row land in the same place:

- Check the inbox: are the same error tags repeating? That's the bottleneck — the app will flag any type over 30% of misses and prescribe an untimed block.
- Drop volume, raise review quality for a week. Twenty questions reviewed properly beat sixty rushed.
- Reread this lesson's first section.

## The final week

The last week before the test protects the score, not raises it. One full test at your real test time of day the weekend before, light level 1–2 drilling, error-journal rereads, and nothing the day before. Your plan already does this; don't override it.
