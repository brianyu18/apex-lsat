## The test you're actually taking

Since August 2024 the LSAT has four 35-minute multiple-choice sections: **two scored Logical Reasoning (LR)**, **one scored Reading Comprehension (RC)**, and one unscored "variable" section that is always another LR or RC and can appear anywhere. Logic Games are gone for good. There is also a separate, unscored 50-minute **Argumentative Writing** sample you do remotely before test day.

That gives roughly 75–78 scored questions: ~50 LR, ~27 RC. Two-thirds of your score is argument analysis. One-third is reading dense prose under time. Everything in this program is weighted the same way.

Since August 2026 nearly everyone tests in person at a test center, on LSAC's LawHub interface. You get proctor-supplied scratch paper, on-screen highlighting, and a flag button. We'll mirror that interface in timed practice so nothing about the screen is new on test day.

## How scoring works

Every question is worth one raw point. No penalty for guessing — **never leave a blank**. Raw points convert to a 120–180 scaled score through an equating table that varies slightly per test. On recent forms:

| Scaled | Misses (of ~77) | Percentile |
|---|---|---|
| 170 | about 8 | 94.5 |
| 173 | about 5–6 | 97.6 |
| 175 | about 4–5 | 98.7 |
| 177 | about 3 | 99.3 |
| 180 | 0–1 | 99.85 |

<KeyIdea>
A 175 is not perfection. It's missing four or five questions out of seventy-seven — about one and a half per section. The game is not "get everything"; it's "stop losing points you already know how to earn."
</KeyIdea>

## What a top score requires

Three things, and we'll measure all of them:

1. **Accuracy on the hard tier.** Each test has 5–8 genuinely hard questions — abstract Flaw, Parallel Reasoning, Sufficient Assumption on conditional chains, science and comparative RC. 165 scorers miss most of these. 175 scorers miss one or two. That accuracy comes from stripping arguments down to their structure, which is what Unit 1 teaches.
2. **Review discipline.** Top scorers can tell you the *exact word* that makes each wrong answer wrong. This program forces that habit: every miss gets tagged with *why* it happened and comes back on a schedule until the pattern is dead.
3. **Skip discipline.** Never spend three minutes on a question you'll probably miss. Mark it, move, come back. Unit 5 trains the decision itself, not just the pacing.

<Callout tone="info" title="What we don't do">
No diagramming every stimulus, no memorized tricks, no 900-lesson slog. Short lessons, a lot of untimed accuracy-first drilling, honest review, and timed sections once the method holds up.
</Callout>

## Your first check

Below you'll do five easy questions, untimed. They're not graded for the lesson — they calibrate the drill engine so it knows where to start you. Pick a confidence level on every answer (sure / leaning / guess); the engine ignores guesses when it rates your skill, so there's no reason to fake certainty.
